# Private car insurance in India: the basics

This explainer covers the general rules that apply to private car insurance in India. For what a specific insurer's policy covers or excludes, search the policy wordings knowledge base.

## The three types of cover

### Third-party (TP) liability only
- Mandatory under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. Driving without it attracts a fine and possible imprisonment.
- Pays for injury, death or property damage you cause to other people. Unlimited liability for death or injury to third parties; property damage capped at Rs 7.5 lakh.
- Does not pay anything for damage to your own car.
- Premium is fixed by IRDAI, not by the insurer, and depends only on engine capacity. Annual rates notified in 2022: up to 1000 cc Rs 2,094; 1000 to 1500 cc Rs 3,416; above 1500 cc Rs 7,897. Electric private cars get a 15 percent discount on these rates.
- Includes a compulsory Personal Accident cover of Rs 15 lakh for the owner-driver unless the owner already holds one.

### Standalone own-damage (OD)
- Covers damage to your own car only. Must be bought alongside a valid third-party policy, which can be from a different insurer.
- Useful when you already have a long-term third-party policy, for example the mandatory 3-year TP policy that comes with a new car.
- Premium is set by the insurer and depends on the Insured Declared Value, car age, model, city and add-ons.

### Comprehensive (package) policy
- Third-party plus own-damage in one policy. This is what most buyers of cars under 10 years old choose.
- Can be extended with add-ons such as zero depreciation, engine protection, roadside assistance, consumables and return-to-invoice.

## What own-damage cover pays for
- Accidental damage, including collision and overturning.
- Fire, explosion, self-ignition, lightning.
- Theft, burglary, housebreaking.
- Natural calamities: flood, cyclone, earthquake, landslide, hailstorm, inundation.
- Riot, strike, malicious acts, terrorism.
- Damage in transit by road, rail, inland waterway, lift or air.
- Flood damage is covered under comprehensive and own-damage policies, but water entering the engine while the car is driven through water (hydrostatic lock) is usually treated as consequential loss and excluded unless an engine protection add-on is bought.

## Standard exclusions in almost every policy
- Wear and tear, mechanical or electrical breakdown, depreciation.
- Damage to tyres and tubes unless the car is also damaged in the same accident, and then only 50 percent of the replacement cost.
- Driving without a valid licence, or under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
- Using the car outside the geographical area (India) or for hire, racing, or other purposes not permitted.
- Consequential loss, such as engine damage caused by driving a car after an accident.
- Contractual liability, nuclear risks, war.
- Loss of accessories by theft unless the car is stolen at the same time, unless an accessories cover is added.

## Key terms

### Insured Declared Value (IDV)
- The maximum amount the insurer pays if the car is stolen or a total loss. It is the manufacturer's listed selling price minus depreciation for age.
- Standard depreciation for IDV: under 6 months 5 percent; 6 months to 1 year 15 percent; 1 to 2 years 20 percent; 2 to 3 years 30 percent; 3 to 4 years 40 percent; 4 to 5 years 50 percent. Beyond 5 years it is agreed between insurer and owner.
- A lower IDV reduces premium but reduces the payout on total loss. Choosing an IDV close to the car's real market value is the usual advice.

### No Claim Bonus (NCB)
- A discount on the own-damage premium for every claim-free year: 20 percent after 1 year, 25 percent after 2, 35 percent after 3, 45 percent after 4, 50 percent after 5 or more.
- One claim resets NCB to zero unless an NCB protection add-on is bought.
- NCB belongs to the owner, not the car. It can be transferred to a new car or a new insurer if the policy is renewed within 90 days of expiry.

### Deductibles
- Compulsory deductible: Rs 1,000 for cars up to 1500 cc and Rs 2,000 above 1500 cc, paid by the owner on every own-damage claim.
- Voluntary deductible: an optional higher amount the owner agrees to bear in exchange for a premium discount.

### Depreciation on claims
- On a normal own-damage claim the insurer deducts depreciation on replaced parts: rubber, nylon, plastic parts, tyres, tubes, batteries and airbags 50 percent; fibreglass 30 percent; glass nil; metal parts by car age from nil for under 6 months up to 50 percent for cars over 10 years.
- Zero depreciation cover removes this deduction.

## Common add-ons and who they suit
- Zero depreciation (nil dep, bumper to bumper): full part cost paid without depreciation. Worth it for cars up to about 5 years old, especially with plastic-heavy bodies. Usually limited to 2 claims a year and generally not offered beyond 5 to 7 years of age. Adds roughly 15 to 20 percent to the OD premium.
- Engine and gearbox protection: covers damage to engine and gearbox from water ingress, oil leakage or hydrostatic lock. Recommended in flood-prone cities such as Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Kolkata.
- Roadside assistance (RSA): towing, flat tyre, battery jump-start, fuel delivery, locksmith. Low cost, useful for highway drivers.
- Consumables cover: pays for engine oil, coolant, nuts, bolts, brake oil, AC gas and similar items used in a repair, which are otherwise excluded.
- Return to invoice (RTI): on total loss or theft, pays the gap between IDV and the original invoice price including registration and road tax. Suits new cars in the first 2 to 3 years.
- NCB protection: keeps the NCB even after 1 or 2 claims in a year.
- Key replacement: cost of replacing lost or stolen keys and locks.
- Tyre protection: repair or replacement of tyres damaged other than in an accident.
- Daily allowance or conveyance benefit: a fixed daily amount for transport while the car is in the garage for an accident repair.
- Passenger cover: personal accident cover for occupants other than the owner-driver.
- Loss of personal belongings: items stolen from the car.

## How a claim works
1. Inform the insurer immediately, usually within 24 to 48 hours of the incident, by phone or app. For theft, also file an FIR.
2. A surveyor inspects the car, either at a network garage or wherever it is parked.
3. Cashless: at a network garage the insurer pays the garage directly, minus deductible and depreciation. Reimbursement: at any other garage you pay first and submit bills.
4. Documents usually needed: policy copy, RC, driving licence, claim form, FIR for theft or third-party injury, repair estimate and bills.
5. Total loss is declared when repair cost exceeds about 75 percent of IDV; the insurer then pays IDV (or invoice price with RTI).

## Renewal rules
- A policy that lapses for more than 90 days loses its NCB. Renewing after expiry usually needs a vehicle inspection.
- New cars sold since September 2018 come with a compulsory 3-year third-party policy; own-damage can be 1 year.
- Switching insurers at renewal is allowed and NCB carries over with a renewal notice or NCB certificate.

## Questions callers often ask
- Comprehensive or third-party? Third-party only is cheapest and legally sufficient, but any damage to your own car is out of pocket. For a car worth more than about Rs 2 to 3 lakh, comprehensive is usually recommended.
- Is zero depreciation worth it? Yes for cars under 5 years old if you want full part costs reimbursed; less useful on an older, low-value car.
- Does the policy cover flood? Comprehensive and own-damage policies cover flood damage to the car. Engine damage from driving through water needs the engine protection add-on.
- Which policy is better for a five-year-old car? Comprehensive with the IDV set near market value; zero depreciation if the insurer still offers it at that age; engine protection in a flood-prone city; skip return to invoice.
- What is excluded? Wear and tear, breakdown, drunk driving, invalid licence, consequential loss, and use outside policy terms.
